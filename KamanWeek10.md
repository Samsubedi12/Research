2 Project Overview
Research objectives
This study aims to investigate how artificial intelligence is used in cybersecurity and how participants perceive its effectiveness. It will explore threat detection, malware identification, phishing prevention and automated incident response. It will examine challenges involving privacy, reliability, bias, explainability and malicious use of AI, and identify priorities for future development.
Research questions
1. What are the main applications of artificial intelligence in cybersecurity?
2. How do participants perceive the effectiveness of AI tools in detecting and responding to cybersecurity threats?
3. What technical, ethical and organisational challenges affect the adoption of AI in cybersecurity?
4. What future developments could improve the responsible use of AI in cybersecurity?
3 Methodology

Research design
The study will use a cross-sectional survey with closed-ended and optional open-ended questions. Quantitative responses will describe participants’ views, while qualitative responses will explain perceived benefits, challenges and future priorities. The survey will measure perceptions rather than independently test AI system performance.
Participant recruitment
Adults aged 18 or older will be recruited through voluntary invitations shared with IT students and IT or cybersecurity professionals through approved academic and professional channels. Screening questions will establish relevant knowledge or experience. Permission will be obtained before posting invitations in institution-managed groups. Participation will not affect academic results, employment or relationships with researchers. No incentives will be offered.
Sample size
Approximately 30 participants will be recruited. This exploratory convenience sample will not be presented as representative of all IT students or cybersecurity professionals.
Data collection methods
An institution-approved online survey will take approximately 10–15 minutes. Questions will cover familiarity with AI and cybersecurity, knowledge or experience of AI security applications, perceived effectiveness, concerns about privacy, accuracy, bias and explainability, and future priorities.
Names, student numbers, employer names, email addresses and confidential security information will not be requested. Participants will be told not to include identifying details in written answers. Automatic email collection will be disabled, and IP address logging will be disabled where supported by the approved platform.
Data analysis plan
Closed-ended responses will be analysed using frequencies, percentages and distributions of rating-scale responses. Written responses will be analysed thematically by identifying recurring ideas. Experience groups may be compared where sizes permit meaningful comparison without increasing identification risk. Conclusions will acknowledge the small sample, convenience recruitment and reliance on self-reported perceptions.

4 Ethical Considerations
4 1 Voluntary and informed participation
A plain-English participant information statement will explain the purpose, eligibility, activities, duration, potential risks, data arrangements and withdrawal limits. It will provide researcher and supervisor contact details and an institution-approved independent contact for concerns or complaints. Technical terms will be explained.
Before accessing survey questions, participants will confirm that they are at least 18, have read the information statement and voluntarily agree to participate. Consent will be recorded using a mandatory checkbox; those who do not consent will not proceed.
Participants may skip non-essential questions or leave before submission without penalty. Incomplete responses will not be retained where the platform permits. After submission, an anonymous response cannot reliably be linked to its author and therefore cannot be withdrawn individually. This limit will be explained before consent.

4 2 Minimising harm and discomfort
Expected risks are low. Discussing cybersecurity concerns may cause mild discomfort. Accidental disclosure of confidential workplace information or identifiable details could create professional or reputational risks. No hacking exercises, malware execution, access to organisational systems, passwords or security logs will be involved.
Questions will focus on general knowledge and opinions. Participants will be instructed not to disclose employer names, client information, vulnerabilities or confidential incidents. They may skip questions or stop. Written responses will be checked and identifying or sensitive details removed or excluded before analysis. Unexpected ethical issues or data incidents will be reported promptly to the supervisor and managed through institutional procedures.

4 3 Protecting confidentiality and privacy
Responses will be stored in an institution-approved location accessible only to the authorised research group and supervisor. Strong passwords and multifactor authentication will be used where available. Research responses will not be uploaded to GitHub, public repositories, personal messaging groups or unapproved AI services. GitHub will contain only the application, research materials and suitable non-identifying summaries.
Broad background categories will be used, and accidental identifiers removed. Platform metadata collection will be checked before recruitment. If anonymity cannot be ensured, the information statement and application will be revised to accurately explain the privacy arrangements.

4 Ethical Considerations Continued

4 4 Ensuring fair and respectful treatment
Recruitment will be open to eligible adults regardless of gender, nationality, cultural background or employment status. Questions will use respectful, neutral language and avoid assumptions about technical ability or culture. Participants will be informed that the survey is in English so they can decide whether they are comfortable participating.
All participants will receive the same information and questions. Lecturers, employers and group members will not pressure participation. Questions will allow positive, negative and uncertain views. Sampling and interpretation biases will be acknowledged, and conclusions will not extend beyond the evidence.

4 5 Honesty with professional colleagues
Findings will be reported accurately, including conflicting views, missing responses and limitations. Data will not be fabricated or altered to support a preferred conclusion. Perceived effectiveness will be distinguished from measured technical performance. Group contributions will be recorded and acknowledged, sources cited, and supervisor guidance recognised. Participants will be thanked collectively without identification. Conflicts of interest will be disclosed.

5 Data Management
Data storage
Research data, consent records and analysis files will be held in institution-approved secure storage. The retention period will follow SIHE requirements and be confirmed with the supervisor before recruitment. Participants will be informed of the confirmed period before consenting.
Data security
Access will be restricted to authorised group members and the supervisor. Strong passwords, multifactor authentication and available encryption will be used. Files will be shared through approved institutional services; unnecessary local copies will be avoided. Reports will contain aggregate findings and checked non-identifying excerpts. Small categories will be combined or omitted where identification risks arise.
Data disposal
After the approved retention period, files will be securely deleted from active storage, local devices and recoverable trash folders. Backups will expire or be removed according to approved service procedures. Printed material will be securely shredded. The responsible investigator will document disposal.
