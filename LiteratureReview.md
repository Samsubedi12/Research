# 4. Common Themes Identified in the Literature

A number of common themes emerge from the 10 selected papers.

## 4.1 AI Enhances Automated Threat Detection

There is a clear consensus that AI and ML can be used to automate cybersecurity threat detection.

Unlike traditional cybersecurity approaches that rely heavily on predefined signatures and rules, ML systems can analyse large quantities of network and security data to detect abnormal patterns.

Studies of zero-day attacks have shown that signature-based approaches may fail to detect attacks for which the relevant signature is unknown. In contrast, ML approaches have the capability to learn behavioural and statistical patterns associated with suspicious activity.

---

## 4.2 AI Improves Detection of New Threats

Several papers discuss the ability of ML and deep learning to detect:

- Zero-day attacks
- Malware
- Anomalous behaviour
- Unknown attack patterns

This is particularly important because cybercriminals continuously change their tactics and techniques. Cybersecurity systems therefore need to identify both known and unknown attack behaviours.

---

## 4.3 Increasing Importance of Deep Learning

The literature identifies several AI and ML techniques used for detecting cybersecurity incidents:

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)
- Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN)
- Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
- Deep Neural Networks (DNN)
- Autoencoders
- Random Forest
- Support Vector Machines (SVM)
- Decision Trees

The literature suggests that AI is not a single technology but a collection of technologies that can be applied to different cybersecurity problems.

---

## 4.4 AI Can Support Human-Like Cybersecurity Tasks

AI is increasingly being used for prediction, detection, analysis and automated incident response.

Applications include:

- Anomaly detection
- Threat identification
- Predictive analytics
- Automated incident response
- Security monitoring
- Threat classification

The systematic review by Ofusoria et al. covers a wide range of AI applications in cybersecurity.

This is directly connected to the research question, which focuses on whether AI can improve the speed of cyber threat identification and response.

---

## 4.5 AI Is a Double-Edged Sword

One of the major themes identified in the literature is that AI can be used for both defensive and offensive purposes.

AI can help defenders:

- Detect cyber attacks
- Identify vulnerabilities
- Analyse large amounts of security data
- Automate security responses

However, attackers can also use AI to:

- Automate cyber attacks
- Improve social engineering
- Evade detection
- Discover vulnerabilities
- Scale malicious activities

Therefore, the effects of AI on cybersecurity are not exclusively positive.

# 6. Research Gaps and Areas Requiring Further Research

Although the literature shows positive results, several significant research gaps remain.

## 6.1 Lack of Standardised Datasets

Different studies often use different datasets, making it difficult to directly compare the performance of AI models.

For example, a model that achieves 99% accuracy on one dataset may not achieve the same performance when exposed to real-world attacks.

Therefore, standardised and realistic datasets are required for meaningful comparisons.

---

## 6.2 Difficulty Detecting Zero-Day Attacks

Although ML is promising for zero-day detection, current approaches still have limitations.

Guo identified limitations involving:

- Accuracy
- Recall
- Consistency across different attacks

Further research is needed to improve the ability of AI systems to detect previously unknown attacks.

---

## 6.3 False Positives and False Negatives

AI-based cybersecurity systems can produce:

- False positives: legitimate activity incorrectly identified as malicious.
- False negatives: malicious activity incorrectly classified as legitimate.

A high false-positive rate can overwhelm security analysts, while false negatives can allow genuine attacks to remain undetected.

Therefore, overall accuracy alone is not sufficient to evaluate an AI cybersecurity system.

---

## 6.4 Explainability

Many advanced AI and deep-learning models operate as "black boxes."

Security analysts may not understand why an AI model classified an activity as malicious.

This creates concerns regarding:

- Trust
- Accountability
- Transparency
- Decision-making
- Automated response

Dhanushkodi and Thejas highlight explainability and resilience as important challenges.

---

## 6.5 Changing Cyber Threats

Cyber attacks continuously evolve.

AI models trained using historical data may become less effective when attackers develop new techniques.

Therefore, cybersecurity models need to adapt to changing attack patterns rather than relying exclusively on historical training data.

---

## 6.6 AI Can Also Be Used by Attackers

The literature increasingly recognises that attackers can use AI to make cyber attacks more sophisticated and scalable.

This creates an environment that can be described as an **"AI vs. AI"** security challenge, where attackers and defenders continuously develop AI-based techniques to counter one another.

# 7. Contribution of the Papers to the Research Question

The 10 selected papers collectively provide evidence to answer the research question:

> **What are the benefits of AI and ML in cybersecurity in terms of better threat detection, prevention of cyber attacks and quicker response time than traditional cybersecurity processes?**

Overall, the literature indicates that AI and ML technologies offer significant potential for enhancing cybersecurity effectiveness through:

- Automated threat detection
- Anomaly detection
- Malware detection
- Intrusion detection
- Predictive analysis
- Zero-day attack detection
- Automated incident response
- Faster analysis of security data

However, the literature also shows that AI is not a complete replacement for traditional cybersecurity approaches.

Traditional security mechanisms such as signatures, rules and established security controls remain important because AI systems can:

- Generate false alarms
- Fail to generalise to new environments
- Be vulnerable to adversarial attacks
- Depend on the quality and age of training data
- Have explainability limitations

Therefore, the literature suggests that AI is most beneficial when combined with existing cybersecurity practices rather than being used as a complete replacement.
