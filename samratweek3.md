# Step 1: Identify and Collect Key Literature

## Paper 1
**AI-Enabled Threat Detection: Leveraging Artificial Intelligence for Advanced Security and Cyber Threat Mitigation**

### Full APA 7 Reference
Dhanushkodi, K., & Thejas, S. (2024). *AI enabled threat detection: Leveraging artificial intelligence for advanced security and cyber threat mitigation*. *IEEE Access, 12*, 1–19. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3493957

### Research Objective
To examine the role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in enhancing threat detection and cybersecurity, with a focus on Industry 5.0, Internet of Things (IoT), 5G networks, and autonomous vehicles.

### Methodology
The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review supported by a tiered experimental framework. Various AI models, including Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), were evaluated in simulated network environments using tools such as CICFlowMeter and Metasploit.

### Key Findings
- AI models achieved detection accuracies ranging from **85% to nearly 100%**.
- GANs effectively solved data imbalance by generating synthetic attack data.
- Explainable AI (XAI) improves transparency and assists human decision-making in industrial environments.
- AI significantly enhances real-time cyber threat detection.

### Common Themes
- Real-time threat detection
- Zero-day attack detection
- Explainable AI (XAI)
- AI adaptation for emerging technologies
- Cyber resilience

### Contribution to Research Topic
This paper provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating different AI architectures in detecting sophisticated cyber threats such as Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) and polymorphic malware. It demonstrates how AI strengthens modern cybersecurity systems.

### Research Gaps
- High computational requirements
- Black-box nature of deep learning models
- Continuous retraining required to maintain model effectiveness
- Limited explainability of AI decisions

## Paper 2
**Next-Generation Cyber Threat Detection and Mitigation Strategies: A Focus on Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning**

### Full APA 7 Reference
Labu, M. R., & Ahammed, M. F. (2024). *Next-generation cyber threat detection and mitigation strategies: A focus on artificial intelligence and machine learning*. *Journal of Computer Science and Technology Studies, 6*(1), 179–188. https://doi.org/10.32996/jcsts.2024.6.1.19

### Research Objective
To investigate how Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning improve cybersecurity and evaluate the effectiveness of AI-based fraud detection using the Feedzai security platform.

### Methodology
The study applied supervised machine learning algorithms, including Random Forest, Naïve Bayes, and K-Nearest Neighbour (KNN), to historical financial transaction datasets to evaluate real-time fraud detection performance.

### Key Findings
- Random Forest achieved the highest detection accuracy of **83.94%**.
- AI significantly improves fraud detection and reduces false positives.
- Machine learning supports automated monitoring and rapid incident response.

### Common Themes
- Behaviour-based anomaly detection
- Financial fraud detection
- Real-time monitoring
- Automated security response
- Machine learning in cybersecurity

### Contribution to Research Topic
The paper demonstrates a practical implementation of AI and Machine Learning in banking cybersecurity, highlighting how intelligent systems improve fraud detection and strengthen financial security.

### Research Gaps
- Dependence on historical datasets
- Human error remains a major security risk
- Social engineering attacks continue to bypass technical controls

## Paper 3
**The Intersection of Artificial Intelligence and Cybersecurity: Challenges and Opportunities**

### Full APA 7 Reference
Sontan, A. D., & Samuel, S. V. (2024). *The intersection of Artificial Intelligence and cybersecurity: Challenges and opportunities*. *World Journal of Advanced Research and Reviews, 21*(02), 1720–1736. https://doi.org/10.30574/wjarr.2024.21.2.0607

### Research Objective
To provide a comprehensive overview of Artificial Intelligence in cybersecurity, including machine learning, deep learning, automation, ethical issues, and future opportunities.

### Methodology
The authors conducted a comprehensive review comparing traditional signature-based cybersecurity methods with AI-driven approaches such as automated threat detection, intelligent scanning, and adaptive risk assessment.

### Key Findings
- AI-powered platforms such as Darktrace and IBM QRadar analyse large volumes of security data far faster than human analysts.
- AI enables predictive analytics and proactive threat detection.
- AI significantly improves scalability and incident response.

### Common Themes
- Intelligent automation
- Predictive threat detection
- Explainable AI
- Ethical AI
- Scalable cybersecurity solutions

### Contribution to Research Topic
The paper provides a broad overview of how AI transforms cybersecurity from traditional rule-based approaches into intelligent, adaptive defence systems capable of responding to modern cyber threats.

### Research Gaps
- AI weaponisation by attackers
- Algorithmic bias
- Privacy and surveillance concerns
- Adversarial machine learning attacks

# Step 2: Read and Analyse the Papers

## Common Themes Across the Three Papers

All three papers highlight that traditional signature-based cybersecurity techniques are no longer sufficient to defend against increasingly sophisticated cyber threats such as zero-day attacks and Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs). They consistently emphasise the importance of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for:

- Real-time threat detection
- Behaviour-based anomaly detection
- Automated incident response
- Predictive threat intelligence
- Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI)
- Improving cybersecurity resilience

Each study demonstrates that AI enables organisations to move from reactive security approaches toward proactive and intelligent cyber defence.

---

## Similarities and Differences in Methodologies

### Similarities

- All three studies examine Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning applications in cybersecurity.
- Each paper discusses supervised and deep learning techniques.
- All recognise AI as a key technology for modern cyber defence.

### Differences

| Paper | Methodology |
|--------|-------------|
| **Paper 1** | Comprehensive literature review combined with experimental evaluation of AI models (SVM, Random Forest, CNN, RNN, GAN). |
| **Paper 2** | Experimental comparison of supervised machine learning algorithms (Random Forest, Naïve Bayes, and KNN) using financial transaction datasets. |
| **Paper 3** | Comprehensive review of AI applications, cybersecurity frameworks, ethical issues, and industry tools. |

---

## Key Challenges Identified

The papers identify several common technical and ethical challenges.

### Technical Challenges

- High computational costs
- Large memory requirements
- Adversarial Machine Learning attacks
- Real-time processing of massive datasets
- Continuous retraining of AI models

### Ethical Challenges

- Algorithmic bias
- Lack of transparency (Black-box AI)
- Privacy concerns
- Mass surveillance
- Responsible AI governance

---

## Research Gaps and Unresolved Issues

The literature identifies several areas requiring further investigation.

- Development of high-quality and continuously updated cybersecurity datasets.
- Improvement of Explainable AI (XAI) techniques to support cybersecurity analysts.
- More robust defences against adversarial machine learning attacks.
- Continuous learning frameworks capable of adapting to evolving cyber threats.
- Ethical frameworks that balance AI effectiveness with privacy, fairness, and transparency.
- Greater validation of AI models using real-world enterprise cybersecurity environments rather than simulated datasets.

---

## How These Papers Support the Research Topic

The three papers collectively demonstrate that Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning significantly improve cybersecurity by:

- Detecting cyber threats more accurately.
- Enabling real-time threat analysis.
- Automating incident detection and response.
- Reducing human error.
- Improving scalability of cybersecurity operations.
- Supporting predictive analytics for emerging threats.

Together, these studies show that AI represents a significant advancement over traditional rule-based cybersecurity systems. However, they also conclude that AI should complement rather than replace human expertise due to challenges including explainability, adversarial attacks, computational cost, and ethical concerns.

---

# Conclusion

The selected literature provides strong evidence that Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning are transforming modern cybersecurity. Although AI-powered systems significantly enhance threat detection, fraud prevention, and automated incident response, several challenges remain, including transparency, computational requirements, ethical concerns, and evolving attack techniques. Future research should focus on developing more explainable, adaptive, and ethically responsible AI-driven cybersecurity systems capable of defending against increasingly sophisticated cyber threats.
